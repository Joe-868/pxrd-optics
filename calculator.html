Applicable configuration
Malvern Panalytical Empyrean with iCore incident optics and dCore diffracted optics (MultiCore), Bragg–Brentano reflection geometry, flat sample. Goniometer radius 240 mm.
Optical components required
iCore with a primary mask (Mask 1) and a secondary mask (Mask 2) from the listed sizes; fixed or programmable divergence slit with a largest opening of 0.61°.
Source of reference parameters
Beam FWHM widths and full axial footprints for each mask pair, divergence slit settings and the automatic-mode relation are taken from the manufacturer's instrument/software Help. Irradiated length uses elementary diffraction geometry.
Assumptions and limitations
  • The mask tables apply to the documented dual-mask optical configuration only. Instruments with other incident optics, or without the corresponding two masks, cannot use these values without verification.
  • The sample-fit check treats the illuminated patch as a rectangle centred on a round sample, using the full axial footprint F so that the whole beam stays on the sample. The FWHM width W is shown for reference.
  • Results are geometric estimates, not measured beam profiles. Weak tails beyond the footprint are not included; verify holder clearance on the instrument.

Explanations

Mask pairs whose footprint fits

Pairs below pass the F × L check at the current DS or target length. Select a pair to fill the iCore masks. Weak tails beyond the footprint are not included.

How these checks are calculated

Mask 1 and Mask 2 select F and W from the manufacturer's tables. For a fixed DS, L at the scan start is L = 240/(sin ω / tan(α/2) + cos ω) + 240/(sin ω / tan(α/2) − cos ω), with α the DS opening and ω = 2θ/2 + offset.

Automatic mode takes a target L of 0.5–20 mm in 0.5 mm steps and sets the DS by the manufacturer's relation α = 1.75 × atan(sin ω / (480/L + cos ω)) (degrees). Settings are capped at 0.61°; beyond that angle L falls below the target, and the estimated L at the stop uses the same relation at 0.61°.

The fit check compares L with √(D² − F²), so the whole beam stays on the sample; √(D² − W²) is shown for reference. A required opening above 0.61° at the stop is flagged.

Why the anti-scatter slit cannot remove every holder peak

The anti-scatter slit limits what the detector sees, not where the beam lands, so a holder reflection on an accepted path can remain. Control the incident illumination and check with a blank holder. MultiCore brochure.
