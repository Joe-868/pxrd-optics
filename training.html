Mask

These diagrams explain the iCore beam path and the three axial beam-width definitions in the instrument guide. They are schematics, not a measured profile or a scale drawing.

Incident path: masks and divergence slit

The pale fan shows rays admitted by Mask 1 spreading before Mask 2. Its outer amber rays strike Mask 2; teal rays pass both masks. The ray spacing does not measure intensity. The iCore guide describes the resulting axial intensity as trapezoidal: core is the flat central width, FWHM (W) is the width at half the peak intensity, and footprint is the full base width. The calculator uses the workbook's FWHM lookup for W. The divergence slit controls irradiated length L in the perpendicular scan plane; its position alone is marked here.

Sample position and holder edge

During a direct-beam Z alignment scan, the sample surface enters the beam until transmission is about half the open-beam value. That height reference is separate from the mask table's W. Positioning the sample there does not halve W or remove weak beam tails.

Sources and limits

The supplied iCore instrument guide (pp. 1–3) defines the trapezoidal profile and lists core, FWHM and footprint widths for each mask pair. The workbook supplies the calculator's FWHM lookup. These diagrams are qualitative. See the XRD alignment guide for sample-height alignment.
