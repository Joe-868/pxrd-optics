General XRD optics

Beam masks

An aperture that limits the beam width across the sample (the axial direction). The diagrams are schematic.

Incident path: masks and divergence slit

A finite source and the admitted divergence give each beam edge a partly lit penumbra; two masks in series are shown, and a single mask behaves alike. Three widths describe the profile: the core (flat top), the FWHM (W) and the footprint (full width to zero). The DS sets L in the perpendicular direction (the scattering plane), so it does not appear in this axial view.

Manuals quote core, FWHM and footprint for specific mask combinations; see the Malvern Panalytical Empyrean calculator, which uses the manufacturer’s tables.

Why the beam is wider than the mask opening

Behind the incident Soller slit, rays still travel at up to about α/2 out of plane. After a mask of opening m they spread further over the mask-to-sample distance d, giving a geometric envelope of

Wfull ≈ m + 2 d tan(α/2)

Assumes an incident Soller slit of full acceptance α, straight rays and no source-size or intensity model. The result is an envelope, not a FWHM; weak tails can extend beyond it. Without a Soller slit the expression does not apply, and that is not α = 0.
