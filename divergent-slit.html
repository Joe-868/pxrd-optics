What changes with a programmable DS?

A fixed DS keeps its angular opening while L changes across the scan. The workbook's automatic setting instead changes the DS angle to target an entered irradiated length.

How to interpret the picture

L and W run in different directions

L is the calculated illuminated length in the scattering plane, controlled here by DS and ω. The Mask guide uses W for the transverse beam FWHM from a mask pair. A sample fit check needs both directions.

Intensity is a tradeoff

A wider DS can admit more incident flux, but overspill can illuminate the holder and add unwanted signal. A variable DS can help high-angle intensity, while changing focusing conditions can affect resolution. These controls do not predict intensity ratios or a measured peak shape.

Workbook geometry and sources

For fixed DS, this page evaluates the workbook PDSCalc!C equation with source-to-sample radius R = 240 mm, DS opening φ in degrees, and ω = 2θ/2: L = 240/(sin ω/tan(φ/2) + cos ω) + 240/(sin ω/tan(φ/2) − cos ω). It approaches R φ(rad)/sin ω when ω is much larger than φ/2. The automatic example evaluates workbook PDSCalc!D: φ = 1.75 × degrees[atan(sin ω/(480/Ltarget + cos ω))].
