Divergence slit

The divergence slit (DS) sets the opening angle of the beam in the scattering plane; with the incident angle, that sets the irradiated length on the sample.

How to interpret the picture

L and W are controlled by different optics

L, the irradiated length in the scattering plane, is determined by the DS and ω. W, the axial width, is determined by the beam mask; see beam masks. A sample-fit check needs both.

Intensity is a trade-off

A wider DS gives more flux but can spill onto the holder, especially at low angles where L is longest. A programmable DS can keep L constant; see fixed, programmable and automatic DS.

Geometric model and sources

Assuming a point source at distance R, a flat sample on the goniometer axis, straight rays and ω = 2θ/2, the limiting rays land at R sin(α/2)/sin(ω ± α/2) either side of the centre, so

L = R/(sin ω / tan(α/2) + cos ω) + R/(sin ω / tan(α/2) − cos ω)

which approaches R α(rad) / sin ω when ω is much larger than α/2. A finite source size and the actual beam profile are not included.
